Monitoring MDRO Patterns through Time-Based Analysis with SQL and Tableau

02Goal

Antimicrobial-resistant infections raise mortality, length of stay and medical costs, so hospitals must continuously monitor and report them. Using microbiology culture results from a university hospital, this project set out to:

  • Count MDRO cases in line with the WHO GLASS standard by removing repeat tests of the same patient.
  • Separate infections acquired in the hospital from those brought in from outside.
  • Build a dashboard where the infection control team can filter by organism, specimen and period and see the current situation right away.

03Methodology & Summary

  • Team scope: The team built an infection response platform (lab report structuring, alert system, chatbot and dashboard). I built the statistics dashboard.
  • Data: Microbiology culture results from a university hospital, structured from free-text lab reports by teammates.
  • Deduplication: A SQL query with window functions keeps only the first test per patient, organism and specimen within one year, following WHO GLASS rules.
  • Dashboard: Three Tableau tabs (Monitoring, Ward Status, Statistics) with filters and KPI cards, built around four surveillance metrics.

04Results

  • Most cases started inside the hospital. In every month analyzed, more MDROs were detected after 48 hours of admission than before, pointing infection control toward transmission inside the hospital.
  • A standard made the counts trustworthy. Counting repeat tests inflates cases; WHO GLASS deduplication produced numbers ready for national reporting.

05From Analysis to Decision

  • See the day's status at a glance. Filters and KPI cards replace manual compilation for the infection control team.
  • Catch rising hospital-acquired cases early through the 48-hour indicator.
  • Feedback: An infection control practitioner said a ward-based dashboard "would make the situation clear at a glance."
  • Limitations: ward data were simulated, the pipeline is not automated, and no usability test with clinicians was done.
