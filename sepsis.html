Predicting Sepsis Mortality through Model Tuning and Threshold Analysis with PyTorch

Predicted the risk of death for ICU sepsis patients from their first 24 hours of data, then lowered the cutoff for flagging a patient as high risk so the model caught more of the patients who died (55% → 80%).

02Goal

Sepsis is a leading cause of death in intensive care units, and how early high-risk patients are recognized strongly affects survival. Using data on 911 ICU sepsis patients provided by the training program (the worst value of each measurement during the first 24 hours after admission), this project asked:

  • Can in-hospital death be predicted from first-day information alone?
  • How should the decision threshold be set so that as few deaths as possible are missed?
  • Which clinical measurements does the model rely on most?

03Methodology & Summary

  • Data: ICU sepsis patient data provided by the training program (911 patients, 270 deaths or about 30%; original source not disclosed), with 34 variables: 23 measurements such as age, blood pressure, urine output and level of consciousness, and 11 yes/no items such as underlying conditions and mechanical ventilation. Split into 637 patients for training and 274 for testing; the test set was used only for the final evaluation.
  • Feature selection (team): The team selected variables in two stages.
    • Stage 1: SHAP importance and VIF were used to drop variables that contributed little or overlapped with others, reducing 50 variables to 34.
    • Stage 2: The team checked which variables ranked high across several models and removed five that were barely used, leaving 29.
    • The top five variables across models were (1) A-a gradient (how well the lungs transfer oxygen), (2) PaO2/FiO2 (blood oxygen ratio), (3) APACHE II (a severity score), (4) lactate and (5) urine output.
  • Team scope: The team compared 12 models: five machine learning models (SVM, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, Random Forest) and seven deep learning models (MLP, ResNet, TabNet, DANet, SAINT, FT-Transformer, TabPFN), and selected CatBoost as the best performer.
  • My role: I trained and tuned two of the deep learning models, using the 34 variables from Stage 1.
    • TabNet is a deep learning model for tables that chooses which variables to look at in each step.
    • FT-Transformer applies the transformer architecture used in language translation to tabular data.
  • Tuning: I used Optuna to automatically search for the best model settings, such as the number of layers and the learning rate. Each setting was scored by five-fold cross-validation, where the training data is split into five parts and each part takes a turn as the validation set, and the setting with the highest average AUC was chosen.
  • Decision threshold: The model outputs a probability of death between 0 and 1, and patients at 0.5 or above are flagged as high risk by default. Because only about 30% of patients died, many of them scored below 0.5 and were missed. I instead chose the threshold that maximized F1 using cross-validated predictions for all 637 training patients, which made the threshold far more stable than choosing it on a small validation set.
  • Evaluation: Model performance was evaluated using:
    • AUC: How well the model separates patients who died from those who survived (1.0 = perfect, 0.5 = no better than chance).
    • Recall: The proportion of patients who died that the model correctly identified as high risk.
    • Precision: The proportion of patients classified as high risk who actually died.
    • F1: A single score that balances precision and recall.
    Each model was trained with five random seeds. AUC is reported as the mean across seeds; threshold-based results use the average of the five seeds' predictions.

Test set of 274 patients (74 deaths). AUC is the mean of five seeds. Recall @0.5 uses the default cutoff; recall, precision and F1 use the cross-validated threshold. Threshold-based values come from the average of the five seeds' predictions.

  • FT-Transformer separated patients better. Its AUC was higher than TabNet's (0.771 vs 0.702), and tuning raised the AUC of both models by about 0.02. At the chosen threshold, however, tuned TabNet had a better balance of precision and recall (F1 0.60 vs 0.54).
  • At the default cutoff of 0.5, nearly half of deaths were missed. The tuned FT-Transformer caught 55% of deaths at 0.5. With the cross-validated threshold (0.22), it caught 80%, and missed deaths fell from 33 to 15. In exchange, more survivors were flagged (37 → 85), and precision dropped from 53% to 41%.
  • A threshold chosen on a small validation set was unstable. When the threshold was chosen on 128 validation patients (39 deaths), it ranged from 0.03 to 0.74 across seeds and gave different recall on different computers. Choosing it from cross-validated predictions for all 637 training patients and averaging the five seeds made the result consistent: 0.20 and 0.22 on two different machines.
  • The two models relied on different variables.
    • To find which variables mattered, I shuffled one variable at a time and measured how much the AUC dropped.
    • TabNet relied most on a severity score (APACHE II), which also ranked third in the team's feature selection.
    • FT-Transformer relied most on level of consciousness (Glasgow Coma Scale, GCS) and whether the patient had a weakened immune system.
    • These are the variables the models used to make predictions, not proven causes of death.

05From Prediction to Decision

  • Who would use it: ICU clinicians, to prioritize monitoring of patients at high risk on their first day.
  • What decision changes: In clinical settings, missing a high-risk patient (a false negative) is far more costly than an unnecessary alert (a false positive), so where the threshold is set matters as much as the model itself. Rather than reporting accuracy alone, I showed in numbers the trade-off between missed deaths and extra alerts, and set the threshold based on it.
  • Limitations and next steps: The data cover 911 patients from an undisclosed source, so performance needs to be checked on data from other hospitals. Even with cross-validation, the tuned FT-Transformer's threshold still varied between individual seeds (0.16–0.52), so threshold results are reported for the five-seed average. Real-world use would require agreeing with clinicians on an acceptable alert level.
