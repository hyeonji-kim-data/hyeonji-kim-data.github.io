Examining Pop-Up Revenue Drivers through Regression and ANOVA in Python

Tested whether high-traffic locations really bring in more revenue for beauty pop-up stores, and found that revenue depended on product price rather than on location or foot traffic.

02Goal

As K-beauty brands open more pop-up stores, choosing where to open them has become a key decision, and busy locations usually come with higher rent. Using 2,133 pop-up events from a public dataset, this project asked:

  • Do locations with more foot traffic actually bring in more revenue, or more revenue per visitor?
  • Does the type of event (for example, a flash event or a mall kiosk) change sales results?
  • Once other factors are held constant, what actually drives revenue?

03Methodology & Summary

  • Data: Kaggle "Luxury Beauty Cosmetics Pop-up Events KPI 2025" (2,133 events, 15 variables), cleaned and grouped by product category and season.
  • RPV (revenue per visitor): revenue ÷ total footfall over the event, counting everyone who visited, not only buyers.
  • Tests: correlation, one- and two-way ANOVA, multiple regression and 95% confidence intervals, each answering one question about location, event type or price.
  • Dashboard: Tableau Public with three tabs (Overview, Brand, Efficiency).

  • Traffic did not bring revenue. High-Street drew 2.04 times the footfall of Art/Design District but earned about 10% less, so its revenue per visitor was $3.63 against $9.92.
  • Price drove revenue. Price alone explained 53% of revenue; location, event type, brand and five other variables added only one percentage point.
  • Event type changed sell-through, not revenue. Flash events sold 4.1 percentage points more of their stock than mall kiosks, with no difference in revenue.

05From Analysis to Decision

  • Choose locations by cost, not traffic. Revenue did not differ by location, so lower-rent sites deserve a look before busy ones.
  • Hit revenue targets through the product mix, such as the share of perfumes and serums, rather than through a prime location.
  • Match the event type to inventory goals. Flash events and mobile trucks suit selling out limited stock.
  • Limitations: revenue is price × units, so price's role is partly built in; the data may be synthetic, and rent costs were not available.
